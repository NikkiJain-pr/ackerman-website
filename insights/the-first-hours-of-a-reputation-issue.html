The first hours of a reputation issue

When something goes wrong, speed matters. Clarity matters more. Here’s what to decide before you say anything publicly.

When an issue breaks, the pressure is to say something immediately. Sometimes that’s right. But a fast statement that later needs correcting can do more harm than the original problem.

Establish what you actually know

Start by separating what’s confirmed from what’s assumed. Write both lists down. Everything you say publicly should come from the first list, and it’s perfectly acceptable to say that you’re still finding out the rest.

Decide who speaks, and who hears first

Agree on one spokesperson and one source of truth. Then think about who should hear from you directly before they read about it: people affected, employees, key customers, partners, and regulators where relevant. Those people deserve to hear it from you.

Say what you’re doing about it

People judge an organization less on what went wrong than on how it responds. A good holding statement acknowledges what’s happened, shows care for those affected, explains what you’re doing now, and says when you’ll update people next. Then keep that promise.

Prepare before you need to

The best time to work through this is when nothing’s wrong. A simple plan, agreed roles and a practised spokesperson will buy you time when you need it most. For specific situations, take legal advice alongside communications advice.
