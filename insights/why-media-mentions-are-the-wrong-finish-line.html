Why media mentions are the wrong finish line

A long list of coverage feels like progress. But the real question is whether anyone important now thinks differently about you.

Most PR reports look reassuring. They list the coverage, add up the reach, and show a line going up. It’s easy to feel good about that, and just as easy to miss the point.

Coverage is an output. What a business actually needs is an outcome: a customer who trusts you sooner, an investor who takes the meeting, a candidate who says yes, a regulator who already knows your name for the right reasons.

Ask better questions

Before chasing a story, it’s worth asking three things. Who needs to think differently about us? What would change their mind? And where do they already pay attention? The answers often point to fewer outlets and sharper stories than a broad campaign would.

One right article beats ten wrong ones

A considered piece in the publication your buyers read every morning can do more than dozens of pickups they’ll never see. It also gives you something lasting: a credible reference your sales team, your website and your future press can all build on.

Measure what the business would recognize

Good measurement starts with the objectives you set at the beginning. Did the right audience hear it? Did they understand why it matters? Did it make the next conversation easier? Those are harder to count than clippings, but they’re the reason to do PR at all.
